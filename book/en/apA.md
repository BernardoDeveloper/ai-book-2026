# Timeline {#sec-apA}

<!-- src: 2026/10/12/llms-agentes-competente-o-suficiente -->
In October 2026, when I redid the AI timeline to write the year's synthesis, it split itself into phases: the parameter wars, the reasoning peak, optimization and the agent era. @sec-ch20 tells that story. This appendix keeps the full list, for reference.

<!-- src: original -->
It has three parts. The first runs from the Perceptron, in 1957, to the launch of ChatGPT, at the end of 2022. The second covers the three years in which the labs traded size for efficiency and the model got hands. The third is 2026 month by month, with what this book tells and the chapter where each thing appears.

The model names are period details, like the car models in a novel. Each date is the one in the chapter that tells the episode, and when the chapter only gives the month, so does this list.

## Before ChatGPT

<!-- src: 2026/02/08/rant-ia-acabou-com-programadores -->
The first artificial intelligence hype is almost seventy years old. The rest of this table is the slow return of the same ideas, up to the [transformer]{.gls} and the first [LLM]{.gls} everybody used.

<!-- src: 2026/10/12/llms-agentes-competente-o-suficiente -->
| When | What | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Jan. 1957 | Frank Rosenblatt describes the Perceptron, a machine that learns by adjusting connections after every mistake [@rosenblatt1957perceptron] | @sec-ch01 |
| Jul. 1958 | The US Navy demonstrates the Perceptron to the press on an IBM 704; the *New York Times* promises a machine that will walk, talk and be conscious of its own existence [@lefkowitz2019perceptron; @sejnowski2020unreasonable] | @sec-ch01 |
| 1966 | Joseph Weizenbaum publishes Eliza, the rule-based chatbot that imitated a therapist [@weizenbaum1966eliza] | @sec-ch01 |
